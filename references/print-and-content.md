# Print and content standards

## Source and coverage

Keep one mapping from official chapter/subtopic to learning point, source page and lesson ID. Source wording is reference material, not a requirement to reproduce textbook pages. Author concise original notes and questions. Record inaccessible sources as inaccessible; do not claim they were read. Use the requested textbook edition and check its contents rather than assuming a different edition has identical subsections.

Keep the answer key beside the content source. A normal four-option question has one defensible correct answer and three meaningful distractors. If multiple answers are intended, say “Select all that apply” and represent the full key. Verify factual claims, units, terminology, diagram labels and translated answers before layout review.

## Page layout

- Use the official chapter/subtopic numbering, then clear lesson titles.
- Put related knowledge in a shared box or region; do not split one concept into decorative fragments.
- Use short clauses with intentional blanks. A blank should test a key term or relationship, not make the sentence hard to parse.
- Keep English and its Simplified Chinese support adjacent. Avoid redundant translation of symbols and short formulas.
- Print the large lesson title on its first page. Continuations can retain a small running header and stable ID.
- Anchor both footer items at the physical page bottom. Use edition/page or lesson identification consistently. Source lists belong in the teacher edition.
- Use at least four choices per Quick check, with visible spacing on every side. Two columns are useful for longer options; four columns only when each option comfortably fits.
- Keep the question stem and all its options together. Do not let a footer, label or answer overlap them.
- Sweep every notes page and every continuation page for useful empty space. Add a full relevant question only when it fits without reducing normal type size or padding.
- Remove “My explanation / sketch” and empty writing lines unless the user specifically requests them. Remove repeated captions that merely announce what kind of illustration is shown.
- Do not remove scientific qualifiers, scale bars or useful figure labels as “extra text.”

Printed page counts may differ by language and by teacher/student edition. Compare content by stable IDs and language, not by an assumption that page 10 means the same thing everywhere.

## Scientific visuals

1. Identify which relationship the visual teaches and which structures need labels.
2. Consult the official textbook and an authoritative scientific reference. Record the reference in the teacher source map.
3. Search for a suitable real photograph or licensed educational image. Record creator, source URL and reuse terms. If unavailable, generate an original teaching illustration.
4. For anatomy, prefer a clean model/cutaway with recognizable geometry and restrained texture. Avoid abstract circles standing in for eyes/ears, graphic flesh and unnecessary microscopic detail. Diagrams and vectors are still appropriate for graphs, arrows and other genuinely schematic material.
5. Generate the base without critical text, then place text and leader lines deliberately. Check each endpoint, spelling, structure relationship and left/right orientation.
6. Inspect the actual printed page and enlarged classroom view. Generation and a citation do not by themselves establish accuracy.

Required attribution takes priority over a clean page. Choose appropriately licensed assets; place any necessary concise credit where the license requires it. Do not move required attribution solely into a private teacher PDF if public reuse terms require it on the displayed work.

## Edition check

Student editions contain learning content and blanks, with no teacher answer key or References appendix. Teacher editions include the answers, teaching notes that materially help, and the references. Keep production commentary, implementation details and verification logs out of both classroom-facing surfaces.
